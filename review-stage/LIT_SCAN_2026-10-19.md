# Literature scan for CADFit++, 2026-10-19

Scope: work from 2024 to 2026 on mesh/point-cloud to CAD-program reconstruction, execution-feedback and validity handling, and tool-using CAD agents, plus techniques we could adopt cheaply.

How IDs were checked: every arXiv ID below was resolved through the arXiv export API (`id_list=`) on 2026-10-19, and the title and submission date were confirmed. Details about method and numbers for CADFather, CADFit, CADBench and StepCAD come from their arXiv HTML pages or project pages. For the other papers, details come from the arXiv abstract only. Venue labels marked "(venue from memory)" were not checked today.

Benchmark naming: in CADBench (2605.10873), **benchA = CAD-All-Ops (ABC)** and **benchE = CAD-Extrude (ABC)**. The other families are B = DeepCAD, F = Fusion 360, M = MCB and O = Objaverse.

## 1. CADFather (the paper the reviewer cited)

**CADFather: Autonomous CAD Reconstruction through Coordinated Tool Use.** arXiv **2610.09127** (v1, submitted 2026-10-06). VERIFIED. Authors: Savrasov, Elistratov, Gavrilov, Garifullin, Pavlov, Kabisov, Frolov, Konushin, Kuznetsov, Zhemchuzhnikov (the same group as CADENA). Code: github.com/kulibinai/CADFather.

**Method.** A frozen VLM assistant (Qwen3.8-27B in FP8, thinking disabled) runs a pool of candidate programs. It decides which candidates to extend, which tool to call, how many proposals to request and when to stop. No model is trained. It has three kinds of tools:
- **Learned proposer.** The CADENA-RL checkpoint, which predicts next operations in a CadQuery-based DSL.
- **Algorithmic proposer.** Described as "conceptually similar to CADFit". On an empty start it proposes a revolve for bodies of revolution and cross-section extrusions from planes that cut the *target mesh*. On an existing candidate it proposes "warm" extrusions: adds for missing material and cuts for extra material.
- **Parameter optimizer.** Numerical gradients on a signed-distance MSE over sketch coordinates, radii and depths. It never changes the structure of the program.

Every proposed or refined program is executed. The assistant sees a candidate table (IoU, GMS, operation count, available tools), up to two candidate renders next to the target render, and messages for failed executions.

**Selection and validity.**
- Score = mean(IoU, normalized GMS), where GMS measures surface and normal agreement. An invalid candidate scores 0.
- A candidate is valid if it builds within 30 s and its *mesh* is a single watertight solid with positive volume. The paper does not mention OCC BRepCheck.
- **Invalid programs are discarded.** There is no repair, no rewriting and no fusing of unions.
- The output is the "protected best" valid candidate, not the last candidate the assistant touched.

**Benchmarks and metrics.** DeepCAD, Fusion360 and MCB (full test sets), CADENA-Bench, CADBench and BenchCAD. Metrics are IoU, GMS, CD (30k points) and IR (invalid ratio). On CADBench the paper also uses the official evaluator: IoU, SIoU, CD and VSR.

**Headline results (IoU / GMS / CD / IR).**

| Benchmark | CADFather | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| DeepCAD | 0.987 / 0.983 / 0.039 / 0% | CADENA-RL sampling: 0.966 / 0.974 / 0.042 |
| Fusion360 | 0.976 / 0.960 / 0.034 / 0% | — |
| MCB | 0.913 / 0.766 / 0.083 / 0% | — |
| CADENA-Bench | IoU 0.909 | — |
| CADBench, own protocol | IoU 0.904, IR 0.7% | — |
| CADBench, official evaluator | IoU 0.930, SIoU 0.787, CD 0.029, VSR 0.968 | CADFit VSR is higher, at 0.981 |
| BenchCAD | IoU 0.948, IR 0% | — |

Per-family CADBench numbers are given only for Base (0.980) and Organic (0.759). **There are no separate A/E (All-Ops/Extrude) numbers**, so we cannot compare directly on benchA-hard or benchE-hard.

**Ablations** (1k-part subsets, change in score):
- Removing the algorithmic proposals costs −0.110 on MCB, and IR rises to 12.4%.
- Removing the optimizer costs only −0.004 to −0.006.
- Learned proposals alone cost −0.114 on MCB.

So the geometric, section-based proposals carry most of the gain.

**Compute.** Per-part budgets are 24 assistant steps, 80 generator samples, 6 algorithmic calls, 10 optimizer calls and 120 executions. Mean wall time is 83 to 307 s per part. On BenchCAD the cost is about $0.046 per part.

**Stated limitations.**
- The optimizer cannot fix a wrong operation sequence.
- Failure cases include a solid block rebuilt as a hollow frame, a missing knurl or shallow pockets (high IoU, low GMS), a spring rebuilt as a tube, and incomplete ribs. The last two are thin-feature failures.
- Revolve-axis detection is not described.

### What CADFather already covers, and where we differ

| Our feature | CADFather | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Execute every candidate, keep the best valid one, score by IoU | Yes (IoU+GMS, invalid scores 0, protected best) | **Pre-empted.** Execution-aware selection is not novel. |
| Agent tools return builds/valid/IoU after model-changing calls | Yes (candidate table with IoU/GMS, failures reported, renders) | **Largely pre-empted.** We differ only in using text-only open LLMs (Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) rather than a VLM with renders, and in giving per-call feedback rather than a pool table. |
| Sections from the original mesh | Yes (the algorithmic tool slices the target mesh) | Pre-empted |
| Execution-verified **delivery**: re-execute the final program, run an OCC BRep validity check | Partial. It checks a watertight single-solid *mesh* and does not mention BRepCheck. | **Differentiator:** a stricter, kernel-level validity check |
| **Fused rewrite** of `.add()` compounds into a boolean union | No. Invalid candidates are discarded. | **Differentiator:** repair instead of discard |
| Fall back to valid alternatives at delivery | Partial (the protected best is always valid, so the effect is similar) | Weak differentiator |
| Revolve-axis fixes | Not described | Possible differentiator if measured |

Implication: position the work as **diagnosis and repair of invalid or compound programs**, together with a controlled tool-versus-policy study. Execution feedback or best-of selection should not be the headline. CADFather is also a must-compare baseline, and its code is released.

## 2. Closest mesh/point-cloud to CAD-program methods

**CADFit: Precise Mesh-to-CAD Program Generation with Hybrid Optimization.** arXiv **2605.01171** (2026). VERIFIED. ICML 2026 poster (seen on icml.cc).
- **Method.** Sketch planes come from clustered planar faces plus axis-aligned slices. Section loops are grouped by containment. A DINO-based prior scores the profiles. Extrude and revolve extents come from a one-sided Chamfer sweep.
- **Revolve axis.** Only full 360° revolves are tried. The candidate axes are the PCA axes of the loop plus four axes rotated 45°.
- **Search.** Greedy forward selection by IoU, then backward pruning, then residual union/cut refinement. Operations that fail to execute are discarded.
- **Results.** On CADBench clean mesh: IoU 0.859, SIoU 0.679, CD 0.038, VSR 0.981. On ABC-Hard: IoU 0.662. It takes about 904 s per shape on ABC-Hard. There is no thin-wall handling.
- **Relevance.** This is our base system. Its axis heuristic is the obvious weak point we already started fixing.

**StepCAD: Mesh-to-CAD Code Generation via LLM Policy and Geometry-Guided Search.** arXiv **2610.03799** (2026-10-01). VERIFIED. Same first author as CADFit.
- **Method.** A Point-MAE encoder plus a Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B policy predicts the next CadQuery operation, conditioned on the target, the current shape and the action history. Each action is executed and the result is fed back.
- **Search.** IoU-guided beam search (K=2) over four local edits per operation: (a) replace the sketch with a profile sliced from the mesh, (b) add a complement cut-extrude, (c) refine parameters with Bayesian optimization, (d) skip the operation.
- **Data and results.** Introduces the ARCADE-1.5M dataset. It is evaluated on CADBench splits and claims up to 87.2% relative IoU gain on ABC-Hard.
- **Relevance.** This is a direct competitor on benchA. The four local edits are a cheap search operator we can copy without the learned policy.

**CADENA: Stepwise CAD Reverse Engineering.** arXiv **2608.00799** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Grows the program one operation at a time and compares the target with the current geometry at each step.
- **Benchmark.** Introduces CADENA-Bench, which is organized by mechanical-part category.
- **Relevance.** It is the learned proposer inside CADFather, and CADENA-Bench is an extra benchmark reviewers may ask for.

**CADReasoner: Iterative Program Editing for CAD Reverse Engineering.** arXiv **2603.29847** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** A trained model iteratively edits a CadQuery program. At each step it receives renders and point clouds of both the target and its own prediction. It also uses a scan-simulation protocol for training and evaluation.
- **Results.** State of the art on DeepCAD, Fusion 360 and MCB, on both clean and scan-sim tracks.
- **Relevance.** A trained alternative to our agent loop. Its scan-sim protocol is a cheap robustness test we lack.

**CAD-Recode: Reverse Engineering CAD Code from Point Clouds.** arXiv **2412.14042** (2024). VERIFIED. ICCV 2025 (venue from memory).
- **Method.** A point-cloud projector feeds a small LLM decoder that emits CadQuery Python. It is trained on 1M procedurally generated sketch-extrude programs.
- **Relevance.** A standard baseline. On CADBench: IoU 0.673, VSR 0.912.

**cadrille: Multi-modal CAD Reconstruction with Reinforcement Learning.** arXiv **2505.22914** (2025). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** One VLM takes point cloud, image or text input. It is trained with SFT, then GRPO-style online RL with programmatic rewards (IoU and validity).
- **Relevance.** A baseline (CADBench IoU 0.687, VSR 0.939). It shows that execution reward improves validity, which overlaps with our validity story.

**CADEvolve: Creating Realistic CAD via Program Evolution.** arXiv **2602.16317** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** VLM-guided edits plus validation grow simple primitives into industrial-style CadQuery generators, yielding 8k parts and 1.3M scripts.
- **Relevance.** A baseline (CADBench IoU 0.707) and a possible source of training data. Its IoU collapses on noisy meshes (0.130).

**CADBench: A Multimodal Benchmark for AI-Assisted CAD Program Generation.** arXiv **2605.10873** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Contents.** 18k samples across six families (B, F, E, A, M, O) and five modalities (clean and noisy meshes, single-view, photorealistic and multi-view renders). Six metrics: IoU, SIoU, CD, VSR, token count and operation count.
- **Relevance.** This is where benchA and benchE come from. Report SIoU and VSR alongside IoU, as CADFather does.

**BenchCAD: A Comprehensive, Industry-Standard Benchmark for Programmatic CAD.** arXiv **2605.10865** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Contents.** 17.9k execution-verified CadQuery programs across 106 industrial part families (gears, springs, drills, and so on).
- **Finding.** Models tend to replace sweeps, lofts and twist-extrudes with plain extrudes.
- **Relevance.** An optional second benchmark. It exposes the operation-set limits that we share.

**Point2CAD: Reverse Engineering CAD Models from 3D Point Clouds.** arXiv **2312.04962** (Dec 2023, CVPR 2024). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Segments the point cloud, fits each segment with an analytic primitive (plane, sphere, cylinder, cone) or a neural freeform surface, then intersects neighboring surfaces to get edges and corners.
- **Relevance.** A B-rep target rather than a program, but its per-segment primitive fitting is the template for snapping our sketch loops and revolve axes.

**Point2Primitive: CAD Reconstruction from Point Cloud by Direct Primitive Prediction.** arXiv **2505.02043** (2025). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** A transformer with explicit position queries predicts sketch curves (type and parameters) and extrusions directly.
- **Relevance.** Low. It is a learned sketch predictor, while our section extraction is already explicit.

**Split-and-Fit (NVD-Net): Learning B-Reps via Structure-Aware Voronoi Partitioning.** arXiv **2406.05261** (2024, SIGGRAPH 2024). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Predicts the Voronoi partition of B-rep primitives, then fits one primitive per cell.
- **Relevance.** Low for programs. It is an alternative way to segment before primitive fitting.

**CAD-SIGNet** (arXiv **2402.17678**, 2024) and **TransCAD** (arXiv **2407.12702**, 2024). Both VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Autoregressive or hierarchical transformers that infer DeepCAD-style sketch-extrude sequences from point clouds.
- **Relevance.** Older baselines from the DeepCAD lineage, worth citing only.

**GenCAD-3D** (arXiv **2509.15246**, 2025). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Contrastive alignment of CAD and geometry encoders, latent diffusion and retrieval, plus SynthBal augmentation for complex shapes.
- **Relevance.** Low. Worth citing as a learned mesh-to-CAD baseline.

**RLCAD: Reinforcement Learning Training Gym for Revolution Involved CAD Command Sequence Generation.** arXiv **2503.18549** (2025). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** An RL gym on a CAD kernel with B-rep input and support for revolve.
- **Relevance.** Prior work on revolve-aware sequence recovery. Cite it next to our revolve-axis fixes.

**Image or text input (cite only):**
- **CADCrafter** (arXiv **2504.04753**, 2025), image to CAD. VERIFIED.
- **CAD-MLLM** (arXiv **2411.04954**, 2024), multimodal conditioning including point clouds. VERIFIED.
- **Text2CAD** (arXiv **2409.17106**, 2024, NeurIPS 2024, venue from memory). VERIFIED.
- **CAD-Coder (VLM, CadQuery)** (arXiv **2505.14646**, 2025). VERIFIED.
- **CAD-Coder (text-to-CAD with CoT and geometric reward)** (arXiv **2505.19713**, 2025). VERIFIED.
- **CADDreamer** (arXiv **2502.20732**, 2025). VERIFIED.
- **Zero-to-CAD** (arXiv **2604.24479**, 2026). VERIFIED. An agentic generate, execute and validate loop that synthesizes about 1M CAD programs.
- **OmniMech** (arXiv **2608.05539**, 2026). VERIFIED. A benchmark that includes a tool-augmented agentic track.

## 3. Execution feedback, validity and tool-using CAD agents

**CAD-Assistant: Tool-Augmented VLLMs as Generic CAD Task Solvers.** arXiv **2412.13810** (2024). VERIFIED. ICCV 2025 (venue from memory).
- **Method.** A VLLM planner runs actions in a FreeCAD Python interpreter. Its tools include a sketch parameterizer, renderers and a 2D cross-section generator, and it adapts to the evolving CAD state.
- **Relevance.** This is the earliest "LLM drives CAD tools including cross-sections" paper. It is prior art for our skill-based agent.

**CADCodeVerify: Generating CAD Code with Vision-Language Models for 3D Designs.** arXiv **2410.05340** (2024). VERIFIED. ICLR 2025 (venue from memory).
- **Method.** A VLM writes validation questions about the rendered object, answers them and corrects the code. Introduces the CADPrompt benchmark.
- **Relevance.** Visual self-verification for text-to-CAD. We have a stronger, numeric verifier (IoU against the input), which is worth stating explicitly.

**CADSmith: Multi-Agent CAD Generation with Programmatic Geometric Validation.** arXiv **2603.26512** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Two nested loops. The inner loop fixes execution errors. The outer loop uses OpenCASCADE measurements (bounding box, volume, **solid validity**) plus a VLM judge.
- **Relevance.** Closest prior art for "OCC validity check plus repair loop", though for text-to-CAD. It weakens any novelty claim for kernel validity checks on their own.

**TraceCAD: Trace-Guided Repair for Agentic CAD Generation.** arXiv **2608.03062** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Keeps persistent links between requirements, operations, failure evidence and repairs. It localizes the faulty operation, searches bounded edits in its dependency region, validates by execution and preservation checks, and stores repair skills.
- **Relevance.** A direct template for a self-repair loop driven by execution errors. Its ablations show that localized search halves regressions.

**Test-Time Scaling for CAD Generation via Verifier-Free Consensus Selection.** arXiv **2608.09706** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Samples N programs, compiles them and returns the one with the highest geometric or topological agreement with the rest of the pool. It is training-free and beats a VLM verifier.
- **Relevance.** Our IoU against the input mesh is a ground-truth-like verifier, so consensus is unnecessary for primary selection. It could still break near-ties or flag likely-wrong topology when the input mesh is noisy.

**DeciCAD: Harness-Grounded Learning of Action Policies for CAD Agents.** arXiv **2610.21755** (2026-10-15). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** A domain-native harness with semantic CAD actions and decision-oriented observations. It cuts output tokens by 45 to 88% compared with a general coding harness, and SFT plus RL improves F1 and IoU.
- **Relevance.** Supports a "harness design matters" framing for our skills. It is a likely reviewer citation.

**Other agentic CAD papers (all VERIFIED; abstracts only, low direct relevance):**
- **CADForge** (arXiv **2610.04262**, 2026). Single-view agent with a review agent, a toolkit built from past failures, and a CAD memory.
- **Vision2CAD** (arXiv **2609.22688**, 2026). Selects geometry by ID through a kernel harness.
- **IterCAD** (arXiv **2606.13368**, 2026). Proposes the CD tolerance-recall metric, which is free of survivor bias. That property matters when invalid outputs are dropped.
- **Seek-CAD** (arXiv **2505.17702**, 2025). Open-model (DeepSeek-R1) self-refinement with VLM feedback. Relevant because we also use open models.
- **CADMorph** (arXiv **2512.11480**, 2025). A plan, generate, verify loop for editing.
- **Teaching LLMs to Self-Debug** (arXiv **2304.05128**, 2023). The generic execution-feedback repair reference.

**MidSurfNet: Learning Face Pairing for Mid-surface Abstraction of Thin-walled CAD Models.** arXiv **2606.01891** (2026). VERIFIED.
- **Method.** Learned pairing of opposing faces for thin-walled B-reps.
- **Relevance.** The face-pairing idea (opposing parallel faces a distance t apart means a wall) is a cheap heuristic for detecting thin walls on meshes.

**Not found:** I found no 2025–2026 arXiv paper dedicated to fillet or chamfer recovery from meshes. Recent fillet recovery appears only inside CADFit (as an operation), in patents (EP 4345673 A1: fit circles along maximal-curvature lines) and in older mesh-segmentation work. Classic references such as Schnabel et al.'s 2007 RANSAC primitives and Jacobson et al.'s 2013 generalized winding numbers are not on arXiv and are cited by their venue.

## 4. Techniques we could adopt cheaply

1. **Revolve-axis fitting from mesh normals.**
   - What: On a surface of revolution every normal line meets the axis. Fit the axis as the line that minimizes the summed squared distance to the normal lines (a linear least-squares or Plücker-coordinate fit), wrapped in RANSAC over triangles. Then snap the result to a nearby principal or world axis if it is within about 2°.
   - Why: It replaces CADFit's PCA ± 45° candidate axes, and neither CADFather nor CADFit describes anything better. It also enables partial-angle revolves (angle from the angular extent of the support).
   - Effort: low, about 100 lines of numpy. The gain is targeted at revolve-heavy parts in benchA, M and BenchCAD.

2. **StepCAD-style local edits on the delivered program.**
   - What: Run beam search (K=2) over existing operations with four edits: (a) re-slice the sketch from the original mesh at that operation's plane, (b) add a complement cut where the prediction exceeds the target, (c) re-tune one operation's parameters with 1-D or Bayesian search, (d) skip the operation.
   - Why: This fixes wrong-structure errors that parameter optimization cannot. CADFather's own limitation is exactly that its optimizer cannot change structure. CADFather's warm add and cut proposals and its −0.11 ablation on MCB show that residual-driven geometric proposals carry the gains.
   - Effort: moderate. We already have IoU scoring and residual cuts.

3. **Repair ladder before discarding or falling back.**
   - What: `ShapeFix_Shape` / `ShapeFix_Solid` → `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` → rerun the boolean with a fuzzy value (`BRepAlgoAPI_*.SetFuzzyValue`) → nudge the coplanar-face offset by ε → our fused rewrite. Re-check with BRepCheck after each step, and log which step fixed what.
   - Why: Every repaired invalid program turns an IoU of 0, or a lower-IoU fallback, into its true IoU. Repair is also our clearest differentiator from CADFather, which discards invalid candidates, and partly from CADSmith.
   - Effort: low.

4. **Thin-wall and sheet handling.**
   - What: Estimate local thickness by casting rays inward along the normal (or by pairing opposing parallel faces, as MidSurfNet does). Where the thickness is below about 3% of the bounding-box diagonal, propose (a) `shell()` on a solid candidate, (b) `offset2D` wall profiles extruded, or (c) section extrusion of thin rib loops with a minimum-width guard so that cleaning does not delete them.
   - Why: CADFather lists hollow-frame, spring-as-tube and missing-rib failures. Thin parts were also flagged in our own review.
   - Effort: moderate.

5. **Snapping section loops to primitives (Point2CAD-style).**
   - What: Fit lines, arcs and circles to section polylines with RANSAC and least-squares circle fits, merge collinear and concentric pieces, and snap radii to the common radius detected across slices.
   - Why: It improves CD and SIoU, makes programs shorter and easier to edit, and reduces sliver booleans, which reduces invalid programs. The IoU gain is small but positive.
   - Effort: low to moderate.

6. **Fillet and chamfer probing.**
   - What: Find sharp-edge candidates in the current solid where the target mesh shows a curvature band. Estimate the radius by fitting circles across the band. Try `.edges(selector).fillet(r)` and `.chamfer(d)` for a few radii, and accept a change only if IoU and SIoU improve and BRepCheck passes.
   - Why: The IoU gain is small, but SIoU, GMS and CD gain more, and these are metrics CADFather reports.
   - Effort: moderate.

7. **Agent self-repair from execution errors (TraceCAD-style).**
   - What: When a tool call fails, return the traceback, the faulty operation index and the last valid prefix. Allow only edits inside that operation's dependency region, and keep a small store of fixes per error type.
   - Why: This mostly reduces wasted calls and tokens with the open models, rather than raising IoU directly.
   - Effort: low.

8. **Boolean-tree simplification.**
   - What: Fold union-of-unions into a single multi-loop extrude, drop operations whose removal changes IoU by less than ε (CADFit's backward pruning, applied after repair), and merge coplanar same-depth cuts.
   - Why: It improves the operation and token counts that CADBench reports, and it removes fragile booleans.
   - Effort: low.

9. **Adding a surface-agreement term to selection and reporting.**
   - What: Score candidates with IoU + λ·SIoU (or GMS) and report SIoU and VSR, matching CADBench and CADFather.
   - Why: IoU alone hides shallow pockets and knurls. This is low effort and matters for reviewers more than for IoU.

10. **Verifier-free consensus (2608.09706) as a tie-breaker only.**
    - What: Use pool agreement to break near-ties under noisy input meshes.
    - Why: Our IoU against the input is already a better verifier, so this is low priority.

## 5. Recommended positioning

- **Do not claim** execution-aware selection or per-call executed feedback as new. CADFather (2610.09127), CADSmith (2603.26512) and CADReasoner (2603.29847) cover them.
- **Claim and measure:**
  1. Kernel-level validity at delivery plus *repair* (fused rewrite and the ShapeFix ladder) instead of discarding.
  2. Controlled ablations on benchA-hard and benchE-hard showing how much IoU comes from repair, axis fixes and original-mesh sections.
  3. A matched-budget comparison of open text-only LLM agents against the deterministic pipeline.
- **Baselines to add:** CADFather (code released, CADENA weights on Hugging Face) and StepCAD, if its code is available.
