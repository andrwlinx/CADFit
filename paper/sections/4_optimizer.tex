\section{Study 1: the optimizer on held-out parts}
\label{sec:optimizer}

\paragraph{Arms.} All arms run frozen source trees on the 300 fresh2 parts. \textbf{d0} is our pre-repair configuration of CADFit, frozen at one commit: on top of the released pipeline it scores compositions by exact mesh union, adds a cut phase, event-driven slicing and direction detection, runs a beam search of width 4, and proposes whole-part section extrusions; two infrastructure fixes common to every arm (a memory bound on mesh decimation with a retry of crashed CadQuery workers, and a seeded surface sampler for the revolve-axis search) are applied to it (exact flags in \cref{app:arms}). \textbf{dx} adds execution-verified delivery: each candidate program is executed and checked, the best valid one is delivered, and an invalid result is fused or repaired (ShapeFix per invalid solid, dropping a solid that stays invalid) before falling back. \textbf{v2} builds section proposals from and scores the search against the undecimated input, and adds three geometry fixes (even-odd loop nesting, keeping loops down to $10^{-4}$ area, densifying revolve profiles at their split); \textbf{v3} adds precise extrusion extents (extent sweep, finer steps along thin directions, ends snapped to cap planes with the unsnapped candidate kept); \textbf{trim} adds a silhouette-trimmed variant (the best valid program intersected with prisms over the target's three axis silhouettes) as one more candidate for the execution check. d0 and dx run once; v2, v3 and trim run with three seeds, averaged within part. The primary contrast, dx~$-$~d0, is analysed alone; the secondary family (Holm over four tests) is v2~$-$~dx, v3~$-$~v2, trim~$-$~v3 and trim against a section-only reference (one executed mid-plane section extrusion per axis, best of three).

\paragraph{Execution-verified delivery.} The baseline d0 obtains a valid replay on \fDZeroValid{} of 300 parts. Of the 41 failures, \fDZeroBuiltInvalid{} programs build an OCC-invalid solid, \fDZeroBuildError{} raise a build error, \fDZeroMissing{} produced no program, and \fDZeroResource{} exceed the replay limits (time or memory); the last class says nothing about the geometry and is scored 0 under the protocol. Delivering the best program that actually builds valid raises gated IoU from \hDZero{} to \hDX{} (\cref{tab:optimizer}), a primary contrast of \hDXvsDZero{} \hDXvsDZeroCI. The gain comes from validity: on parts where both arms deliver a valid program the mean difference is \hDXvsDZeroBothValid.

\paragraph{Later stages.} Each later stage adds a smaller, significant increment: \hVTwovsDX{} for the geometry fixes, \hVThreevsVTwo{} for precise extents, and \hTrimvsVThree{} for the trim (better/worse \hTrimvsVThreeWL). These adjusted $p$-values are Holm over the three available tests and are provisional until the fourth (trim against the section-only reference) is computed. The trim records are the trim stage's own replays, which use a 600\,s limit instead of the common 900\,s; a common-protocol replay of the delivered trim programs has not been run. The trim arm also re-runs the fuse/repair pass before trimming. Over all 300 parts and per seed, that pass changes gated IoU by \hRepassLo{} to \hRepassHi{} (the negative value is one seed-0 replay that timed out inside the trim run and is kept as a frozen 0), and the trim itself adds \hTrimProperLo{} to \hTrimProperHi.

\paragraph{Recovery amendment.} The first scoring pass stored replay failures that were not evaluations: on one node the sandbox could not be set up (dx: 299 parts), and one v2 seed hit the 900\,s limit on 222 parts that the other seeds replayed. After those outcomes were visible we adopted, for all eight runs, a rule giving every setup or resource-limit failure one terminal recovery evaluation with the same judge and limit; programs were not regenerated. It affected \hRecoveredTotal{} records. d0's ten resource failures failed again and stay 0; dx and the v2 seed recovered. On the original records the primary contrast is \hPreDXvsDZero{} (dx valid on \hPreDXValid{} part) and the three-seed v2 mean is \hPreMeanVTwox{} instead of \hMeanVTwox. Both analyses are in \cref{app:recovery}; we report the recovered one as the measurement and label it as amended.

\paragraph{Runtime.} Execution checking costs time: mean minutes per part rise from \hMinDZero{} (d0) to \hMinDX{} (dx) and \hMinVThree{} (v3); per-run medians from \hMedDZero{} to \hMedVThree. These are successful-attempt wall times on CPU nodes that usually ran three parts at once; retried attempts are excluded, so they are not total compute. The trim run's timer starts after its initial replay and repair, so its full latency is not measured.

\paragraph{Pending outputs.} Three preregistered analyses are still running: the section-only reference and with it the fourth secondary test (\hSection), which tests the trim package against a simple geometric reference; a geometric re-scoring of every delivered tessellation with a different seed and four times the samples (\hRejudge), which tests the stability of the IoU estimates; and surface agreement on the fused boundary (\hSurface), which tests whether the volumetric gains preserve boundary fidelity. The contrasts in \cref{tab:optimizer} are computed from the first scoring pass.
